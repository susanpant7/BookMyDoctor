# Specialties

## Cardiology – what it covers
Cardiology is the medical specialty for the heart and blood vessels. A cardiologist treats high blood pressure (hypertension), high cholesterol, chest discomfort that is not an emergency, palpitations and irregular heartbeat (arrhythmia), heart failure follow-up, breathlessness on exertion, leg swelling related to the heart, and heart checkups before surgery. Cardiology also offers preventive heart health reviews for people with a family history of heart disease.

## Cardiology – common tests
A cardiologist may order an ECG (electrocardiogram), an echocardiogram (heart ultrasound), a stress test, a lipid profile blood test, or a 24-hour Holter monitor that records the heartbeat over a full day.

## Cardiology – how to prepare
Before a cardiology visit, bring a list of all current medicines and any previous heart test reports. If a lipid profile (cholesterol) blood test is planned, do not eat for 9 to 12 hours before the test; plain water is allowed. Bring home blood pressure readings if you have them.

## Dermatology – what it covers
Dermatology is the medical specialty for the skin, hair and nails. A dermatologist treats acne, eczema, psoriasis, skin rashes, hives, fungal skin infections, warts, moles that have changed in size or color, hair loss, dandruff, nail infections, rosacea, sun damage, and skin allergies. Dermatology also offers yearly full-body skin checks to screen for skin cancer.

## Dermatology – how to prepare
Before a dermatology visit, do not wear makeup, nail polish or creams on the area to be examined. Bring photos if a rash comes and goes, and a list of skin products and medicines already tried. For a mole check, note when the mole first appeared or changed.

## Pediatrics – what it covers
Pediatrics is the medical specialty for babies, children and teenagers up to age 18. A pediatrician treats childhood fever, cough and cold, ear infections, sore throat, stomach upset and diarrhea, childhood asthma, allergies, feeding problems, growth and development concerns, and bedwetting. Pediatrics also covers newborn checks, well-child checkups, school and sports physicals, and childhood vaccinations. Adults over 18 should see General Practice instead.

## Pediatrics – how to prepare
When bringing a child to a pediatric visit, bring the child's vaccination record, a list of medicines with doses, and notes of temperature readings if the child has a fever. For newborns, bring the hospital discharge papers. A parent or legal guardian must attend with any patient under 18.

## Orthopedics – what it covers
Orthopedics is the medical specialty for bones, joints, muscles, ligaments and tendons. An orthopedic doctor treats back pain, neck pain, knee, shoulder and hip pain, joint stiffness, arthritis, sports injuries, sprains and strains, tendonitis, carpal tunnel syndrome, plantar fasciitis (heel pain), and follow-up care after fractures. Orthopedics also gives advice on physiotherapy and recovery exercises.

## Orthopedics – how to prepare
Before an orthopedic visit, wear loose clothing so the painful area can be examined easily, and bring any X-ray, MRI or CT scan reports and images.

## General Practice – what it covers
General Practice (family medicine) covers everyday health problems in adults and is the first point of contact for most concerns. A general practitioner treats fever, cold and flu, cough, sore throat, headache and migraine, stomach pain, nausea, diarrhea, urinary infections, tiredness, mild allergies and minor injuries. General Practice also manages long-term conditions like diabetes and thyroid problems, does yearly health checkups, routine prescription refills and adult flu shots, writes medical certificates, and refers patients to specialists.

## General Practice – when to start here
If a patient is not sure which specialty they need, General Practice is the best place to start. A general practitioner can examine the patient, order basic tests, and refer the patient to Cardiology, Dermatology, Orthopedics or Pediatrics if specialist care is needed. A referral is not required to book a specialist at BookMyDoctor.

## Choosing between General Practice and a specialist
For a new problem that is not clearly about the heart, skin, bones or a child, start with General Practice. Go directly to a specialist when the problem clearly belongs to that specialty: a skin rash to Dermatology, knee pain after sport to Orthopedics, palpitations to Cardiology, or a sick child to Pediatrics.

## Specialties BookMyDoctor does not offer
BookMyDoctor does not offer neurology, psychiatry or psychology, gynecology, dentistry, eye care (ophthalmology), ENT (ear, nose and throat), or oncology. For these needs, patients can book General Practice for initial advice and a referral to an outside specialist.
